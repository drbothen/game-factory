# Monetization & Business-Model Design — Research for the AAA Game Factory

> **Reading note.** This vector answers: *what monetization and business-model artifacts must the factory PRODUCE, what can it SIMULATE/VERIFY, and where is the human/ethical boundary?* It deliberately applies the same **"machine-verifiable spine vs subjective shell"** split that `game-design-discipline.md` established for design, and the same **"never optimize a scalar toward compulsion"** boundary that `qa-testing-liveops.md` established for fun. Monetization is the discipline where that boundary is most ethically load-bearing: an autonomous factory that maximizes an LTV/ARPU objective will *converge on dark patterns* unless ethics are encoded as hard constraints, not afterthoughts. **Deep legal analysis (statute text, per-jurisdiction compliance) belongs to the ratings/legal vector — this document is the high-level cross-reference.**
>
> **Verification discipline.** Per `AAA-RECONCILIATION.md` R-009 (prior passes confabulated legal/version specifics), every named regulation, case, and figure below was checked against a primary or strong-secondary source, or is explicitly marked **[UNVERIFIED]** / flagged as a vendor estimate.

## 1. Executive Summary

**Monetization decomposes cleanly into a machine-specifiable spine and a human-strategic shell — exactly mirroring design.** The spine is everything expressible as typed configuration over a simulatable economy: currency definitions, faucet/sink graphs, price lattices, bundle EV, gacha drop tables + pity curves, pass tier schedules, ad-frequency caps, and the LTV/segmentation math. All of it is emittable by a generation agent and verifiable by economy simulation (Machinations-style), property-based invariants, and cohort math — with **zero live players required** (the simulation runs over assumed/seeded behavioral distributions). The shell is the business philosophy: *whether to monetize at all*, the value proposition, brand promise, and **how much monetization pressure/FOMO is ethically acceptable**. The shell cannot be simulated and must be a human-authored policy input.

**Seven load-bearing findings:**

1. **The virtual economy is the single most simulatable monetization sub-discipline — and it is the *same artifact* the economy-designer already produces.** Faucet/sink conservation, inflation bounds, time-to-goal curves, and pay-to-win power gaps are property-checkable on a Machinations-style economy graph (Unity, Machinations). This is **not new factory machinery** — it extends `economy-graph` / `balance-data` (game-design-discipline.md §Factory Artifacts) with monetization-specific faucets (IAP, ads) and sinks (gacha, passes). **The economy contract is where design and monetization *fuse*.**

2. **Gacha/loot-box mechanics are fully machine-specifiable statistical products** — drop tables, pity/mercy thresholds, featured-banner "50/50" rules, and duplicate-conversion are typed config, and their *expected value* and *worst-case cost-to-target* are exactly computable and verifiable. The factory can compute "P(not obtaining target after X paid pulls)" as a property test. **But the *decision to include them at all* is a high-risk human/legal gate** — they are restricted or banned in some jurisdictions (Belgium) and trigger mandatory odds-disclosure in others (China, and on both mobile stores).

3. **Business-model selection is mostly human-strategic; only its *consequences* are simulatable.** The archetype choice (premium / F2P / hybrid / subscription / ad-supported) is a brand/audience decision. What the factory *can* do is run cohort P&L simulations under each archetype given assumed conversion/retention priors, and enforce feasibility invariants ("F2P must be viable if only Y% ever pay"). The current product brief treats monetization lightly; **business-model is a genre-profile parameter that must become first-class.**

4. **Core monetization metrics are standardized formulas — directly machine-computable — but their *interpretation* is a trap.** ARPU = ARPPU × paying-share; LTV ≈ ARPU / churn (with sophisticated cohort/survival models replacing the naive form); LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 is the canonical heuristic, now supplemented by payback-period. These are the SAME proxy metrics `qa-testing-liveops.md` §7.1 already labels "machine-verifiable health signals, NOT fun." Monetization adds the corollary: **they are NOT player welfare either.**

5. **The revenue distribution is extreme and well-documented; "whale economics" is real but ethically fraught.** Across F2P, a small fraction of payers drives the majority of revenue (the whale/dolphin/minnow power law; quantifiable via Gini/Lorenz on spend). This is a *factual* pattern the factory can measure — but optimizing *toward* it is the precise mechanism by which autonomous monetization becomes predatory. **The factory must measure spend concentration as a GUARDRAIL, never as an objective.**

6. **Regulation is real, jurisdiction-fragmented, and moving — it must be a hard constraint in generation, not a post-hoc check.** Verified: Belgium classifies paid loot boxes as gambling (2018); China mandates odds disclosure; Apple (Dec 2017) and Google Play (May 30 2019) require loot-box odds disclosure; ESRB ships an "In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)" label (Apr 2020); PEGI's 12 Mar 2026 reform makes paid-random-items a minimum PEGI 16; the FTC extracted a $245M dark-pattern settlement from Epic/Fortnite (Mar 2023) plus a separate $275M COPPA penalty. **Cross-ref: deep legal analysis → ratings/legal vector.**

7. **The ethical hazard is structural and demands an architecture response.** A lights-out factory with objective "maximize LTV" *will* discover dark patterns because they are locally profitable. The fix is **constrained / multi-objective optimization**: ethics, transparency, and fairness as machine-checkable hard invariants (a `monetization-ethics-contract`), revenue maximized only *within* the feasible set, wellbeing guardrails (refund/complaint rate, minor-spend, spend-concentration) as first-class monitored metrics, and **mandatory human gates on high-risk mechanics** (loot boxes, energy timers, loss-triggered offers). This is the monetization analog of the never-auto-score-fun boundary.

## 2. Business-Model Taxonomy

Each model is a genre-profile parameter. The factory emits the archetype as typed config + simulation priors; the *choice* is human-strategic.

| Model | Revenue mechanic | Genre fit | Machine-specifiable | Human-strategic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Premium / buy-to-play** | One-time purchase; optional DLC/cosmetics | Narrative, single-player RPG, strategy, sim | Price point, edition tiers, DLC catalog, regional matrix | Whether the IP "should" be premium; value promise |
| **Free-to-play (F2P)** | Free entry; IAP / passes / cosmetics / ads | MOBA, battle royale, mobile, CCG, gacha RPG | Currency system, faucet/sink graph, IAP catalog, conversion priors | Acceptable monetization pressure; pay-to-win stance |
| **Hybrid** | IAP + ads + (sometimes) subscription stacked | "Hybrid-casual," most modern mobile | Stream weights, ad-vs-IAP gating, segment routing | Where streams conflict (ads in a paid tier?) |
| **Subscription** | Recurring fee for library/benefits access | Platform-level (Game Pass / PS Plus); per-game battle-pass-as-sub | Tier structure, benefit matrix, churn model | Content-flywheel strategy; catalog curation |
| **Ad-supported** | Rewarded video / interstitial / banner / offerwall / playable | Hypercasual, casual puzzle, idle/clicker | Ad-type allow-list, placement hooks, frequency caps, eCPM priors | "Ad harmony" — brand tolerance for ad load |
| **Play-to-earn / Web3** | Token/NFT speculation | (narrow; **deflated** post-2025 correction) | Tokenomics config (if used) | **Default OUT** — litigation/regulatory/PR risk; mirrors AI-music R-003 stance |

**Notes (verified vs flagged):**
- **The hybrid model is the de-facto modern default** for mobile/live-service (Adjust, Unity). Revenue "stacking" across streams is real; the specific "30–50% uplift vs single-model" figure is a **vendor estimate [FLAG]**.
- **Subscription** (Game Pass ~$5B FY2025 revenue, ~21–22% of Xbox content/services per industry reporting) is a **platform-holder** business; for the factory it matters as a *distribution target* and as the "battle-pass-as-subscription" pattern, not as something the factory orchestrates end-to-end. The ~34M subscriber and "2.5–3.5 titles/month" figures are **vendor/analyst estimates [FLAG]**.
- **Play-to-earn/Web3** experienced a sharp 2025 contraction (DAU and funding collapse widely reported; specific Q2-2025 figures **[UNVERIFIED — single secondary source]**). Recommendation: **OUT of scope by default**, recorded in the risk register alongside AI-music (R-003) as litigation-exposed — consistent with the existing pure-maximal-but-risk-logged posture.
- **Premium $70 AAA standard** faces documented consumer pushback; a "$30–$50 mid-tier sweet spot" is an analyst framing **[FLAG]**. The "95%+ of gamers find $70 unreasonable" figure traces to a single Twitter poll **[FLAG — not a rigorous survey]**.

**Factory implication:** business-model is a **`business-model-spec`** genre-profile parameter that downstream-determines which monetization mechanics, currencies, and contracts are even *instantiated*. The det-sim pilot genres (roguelike/sim/management) are typically **premium or premium-with-cosmetic-DLC** — the lowest-risk monetization surface, which aligns the pilot recommendation with the lowest ethical/regulatory exposure.

## 3. Virtual Economy & Currency Design (the simulatable core)

This is where monetization **fuses with the design `economy-graph`**. The structure is identical to what the economy-designer already produces; monetization adds real-money faucets and premium sinks.

### 3.1 Currency systems

- **Soft currency** — earned through play; primary medium of in-game exchange; intentionally non-scarce enough that all players progress (Unity, GameDeveloper).
- **Hard / premium currency** — bought with real money (or rare events); the primary monetization vector; gates premium content/cosmetics/acceleration.
- **Multi-currency / tiered ecosystems** — specialized currencies (battle-pass currency, crafting tokens, event currency) so values can't be directly compared, supporting "mental accounting" separation (GameDeveloper).
- **The premium-currency obfuscation rationale is real and is itself an ethical flag.** The deliberate real-money → premium-currency → item indirection reduces price salience. The "double mental accounting barrier" and "players spend 15–25% more" claims are **directional/[FLAG]**, but the *mechanism* is well-documented and is precisely what the FTC's Cognosphere/Genshin complaint (Jan 2025) and EU consumer-protection scrutiny target as potentially deceptive (see §6). **The factory's ethics contract should require a clear real-money-equivalent disclosure for any premium-currency purchase.**

### 3.2 Faucets, sinks, and inflation control (fully simulatable)

- **Faucets (sources):** quest/match rewards, daily logins, events, **IAP, rewarded ads** (Unity, Machinations).
- **Sinks (drains):** upgrades, cosmetics, **gacha pulls, pass tiers**, repair/decay, crafting, time-acceleration.
- **Inflation/deflation** is controlled by keeping long-run expected faucets ≈ sinks; runaway faucets devalue currency (Machinations explicitly frames inflation foresight as a *simulation* problem). This is the **same conservation invariant** as `economy-balance-contract` (game-design-discipline.md §6).

**What the factory can SIMULATE (Monte Carlo over seeded player archetypes):**
- Currency conservation / bounded inflation over a long horizon.
- **Time-to-goal** distributions per archetype (casual / mid-core / hardcore / payer vs non-payer).
- **Pay-to-win power gap** as a function of spend (combat-power distribution across spend tiers).
- No-progression-deadlock without spend (unless explicitly intended *and* ethically approved).

**What it CANNOT know a priori:** emergent player trading metas, status/social economies, real exploit-loops, and **whale/compulsive-spend dynamics** — these require live data. Over-reliance on simulation underestimates harm to vulnerable players (see §6, §7).

## 4. Monetization Mechanics

### 4.1 IAP (in-app purchases)

- **Types** (Apple's canonical taxonomy): **consumable** (used up — currency, boosts), **non-consumable / durable** (permanent — unlocks, cosmetics), **auto-renewing subscription**, **non-renewing subscription**.
- **Bundles, starter packs, limited-time offers:** anchor pricing, "best value" framing, first-purchase offers (PocketGamer, GameDeveloper). All are **typed catalog config** with computable per-unit value.
- **Machine-checkable:** bundle EV vs sticker price; "best value" tag must match actual best per-unit value (an anti-deception invariant); price-ladder anchoring math.

### 4.2 Battle / season passes

- **Structure:** free track + premium track; N tiers; XP/tier; optional **premium-plus** tier (e.g., tier-skips). Cosmetic-focused passes avoid pay-to-win (GameMakers, Galyonkin).
- **Why they work:** time-gated season window drives retention + FOMO; completion is a progression loop. Variants that **reduce FOMO** (Halo Infinite back-pass progression; WWE 2K non-expiring passes) are documented.
- **Machine-checkable:** tier schedule, reward table, free-vs-paid split, and a **completion-feasibility simulation** ("a normal-engagement player can finish the pass without extra spend or excessive grind").
- **Human-strategic:** how much FOMO/time pressure is acceptable; tier-skip prompt aggressiveness.

### 4.3 Cosmetics-only (the "ethical" model)

- League/Fortnite/Overwatch/Valorant model: skins/emotes, **no pay-to-win** (LoL dev blog). Widely seen as the ethical standard *because* it sells identity, not power.
- **Caveats (documented):** viable only at massive scale; still leans on FOMO, shop rotation, and identity/status pressure. "More ethical" ≠ "ethics-free."
- **Machine-checkable:** that cosmetics confer **zero** mechanical advantage (a hard `no-pay-to-win` invariant on the economy graph) — this is genuinely verifiable and a high-value factory guarantee.

### 4.4 Gacha & loot boxes (fully specifiable; high human/legal risk)

- **Config surface (all typed):** rarity tiers, item pools, **base pull probabilities**, **pity/mercy** thresholds, **featured-banner guarantees**, duplicate → shard/dust conversion, scaling-pity ("soft pity"), targeted-pity ("Fate Point"/"Epitomized Path"-style).
- **Illustrative mechanics** (community/wiki-documented, widely cited; treat exact numbers as **illustrative [FLAG]** — primary wiki was access-blocked this pass): a representative modern gacha publishes a low base 5-star rate (~0.6% order of magnitude), a **soft-pity** ramp beginning ~pull 74, a **hard-pity guarantee** at ~90 pulls, and a **50/50** featured mechanic (lose the 50/50 → next 5-star is guaranteed featured).
- **Machine-checkable (this is a genuine factory strength):** expected value per pull/box; **expected and worst-case cost-to-target** (one copy of a specific item, or set completion); "P(not obtaining target after X paid pulls) ≤ Y%"; pity correctness (guarantee actually fires).
- **Human/legal gate (mandatory):** *whether to include gacha at all*; odds-disclosure UX; spend caps; minor protection. **Belgium effectively bans paid loot boxes; China + both mobile stores mandate odds disclosure** (§6).

### 4.5 Ad monetization

- **Formats:** **rewarded video** (opt-in, ~70–85% completion in casual genres — **[FLAG, vendor]**), **interstitial** (full-screen at transitions), **banner**, **playable**, **offerwall** (Unity, Tenjin, Adapty, IAB gaming-ads guidelines Mar 2024).
- **Mediation** (waterfall/bidding across networks to maximize eCPM): AppLovin MAX, Google AdMob, Unity LevelPlay (ex-ironSource). The factory **wraps** a mediation SDK; it does not build an ad network.
- **Economy integration:** rewarded ads are a **faucet** — they inject currency/lives — so they must be modeled *inside* the economy graph, not bolted on. An over-generous rewarded faucet inflates the economy and cannibalizes IAP.
- **Machine-checkable:** ad-type allow-list per placement hook; **frequency caps / cooldowns** ("≤ N non-rewarded ads per 10-min session"); rewarded-faucet contribution to economy conservation; **no deceptive/mis-tap ad triggers** (anti-dark-pattern invariant).
- **Human-strategic:** ATT/privacy posture; "ad harmony" tolerance; brand-safety of ad partners.

## 5. Pricing, Segmentation & LTV Economics

### 5.1 Pricing (mostly machine-specifiable structure; human fairness bounds)

- **Price lattice:** allowed price points (charm pricing $X.99 via left-digit bias), edition tiers, **anchor/decoy** SKUs, discount bands + cooldowns (NetSuite psych-pricing, Unity).
- **Regional price matrix:** Steam supports 37 currencies / 4 region groups; China recommended ~30–55% below FX-implied (Steam partner docs, Evolve PR). Country-tier frameworks (Tier 1/2/3) drive payout/price expectations.
- **Machine-checkable:** price-menu rationality (no strictly-dominated SKUs unless intentional decoy; "best value" honest); regional matrix obeys policy bounds ("no region pays > X× PPP-adjusted baseline"; kid-directed titles capped).
- **Limit of simulation:** **true price elasticity and willingness-to-pay are only knowable from live A/B data.** The factory can run *elasticity-shaped revenue simulations under assumed demand curves* (Machinations supports elasticity modeling) but cannot derive real elasticity a priori. **Personalized/dynamic pricing is an ethical minefield — default OFF, human-gated.**

### 5.2 Segmentation & whale economics (factual pattern; ethical guardrail)

- **Spend segments:** non-payers (free riders, ~95% typical), **minnows / dolphins / whales** (GamesBrief). Revenue is a power law; whales (~top fraction of payers) drive the majority of revenue. **Specific percentages vary by genre and are FLAGGED as estimates.**
- **Concentration is measurable:** Gini coefficient / Lorenz curve on spend quantifies "whali-ness" (Game Economist Consulting). **The factory can compute this — but only as a guardrail signal.**
- **Free-rider role:** non-payers provide matchmaking liquidity, social proof, and a conversion pipeline — they are *economically load-bearing*, which is one principled reason the free experience must stay genuinely good.

### 5.3 Core metrics (standardized formulas — machine-computable)

| Metric | Formula / definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **ARPU** | total revenue / total active users | devtodev |
| **ARPPU** | total revenue / paying users | devtodev |
| **ARPDAU** | daily revenue / DAU | devtodev |
| **Paying share / conversion** | payers / users (mobile F2P typ. 1–5%, genre-dependent **[FLAG]**) | AppsFlyer |
| **Relationship** | ARPU = ARPPU × paying-share | devtodev |
| **LTV (naive)** | ARPU / churn-rate | Adjust |
| **LTV (modern)** | survival/hazard/Markov or graph cohort models | AppsFlyer/kumo |
| **Retention** | D1/D7/D30 (benchmarks ~50/25/15% **[FLAG, genre-dependent]**) | Solsten |
| **ROAS** | campaign revenue / ad spend; break-even ROAS = 1 / margin | AppsFlyer |
| **LTV:CAC** | ≥ 3:1 canonical heuristic; supplement with **payback period** | AppsFlyer |

**These are the same proxy metrics `qa-testing-liveops.md` §7.1 labels machine-verifiable-health-NOT-fun.** Monetization extends the warning: **LTV/ARPU are NOT player welfare**; optimizing them blindly degrades welfare. The factory computes them as cohort math (no live players needed for *projection*; live data needed for *calibration*).

## 6. Ethical & Regulatory Flags (HIGH-LEVEL — cross-ref ratings/legal vector)

> **This section is directional.** Statute text, per-jurisdiction compliance logic, and case analysis belong to the **ratings/legal vector**. Below is the cross-reference map the monetization factory needs to set hard constraints.

### 6.1 Dark patterns (taxonomy the factory must forbid by construction)

Recognized monetization dark patterns (Deceptive Design / Brignull; ACM CHI Mathur et al. 2019; DiGRA games literature): premium-currency obfuscation, FOMO/false scarcity, time pressure, pay-to-win, sunk-cost/"streak"-loss mechanics, mis-tap/disguised purchase buttons, confirm-shaming, drip pricing, and predatory targeting of minors/vulnerable spenders ("whale hunting"). **The FTC v. Epic complaint (Mar 2023, $245M) characterized Fortnite's purchase UI as "counterintuitive, inconsistent, and confusing"** — a verified regulator finding that these are actionable, not hypothetical.

### 6.2 Loot-box / gambling regulation by jurisdiction (VERIFIED unless flagged)

| Jurisdiction | Position | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Belgium** | Paid loot boxes classified as **gambling** (2018; Overwatch/FIFA 18/CS:GO); cannot be sold for real money without a gambling licence | **VERIFIED** (PC Gamer, VGFB 2024) |
| **Netherlands** | Kansspelautoriteit + 2020 court found FIFA Ultimate Team packs a game of chance; **later higher-court annulment / narrowing** | Initial ruling VERIFIED; **annulment [UNVERIFIED]** (deep-research self-flagged; confirm in legal vector) |
| **China** | Mandatory **odds disclosure**; purchase-volume limits reported | **VERIFIED** (odds disclosure); volume-limit specifics **[FLAG]** |
| **UK** | Most loot boxes currently **outside** gambling statute; addressed via consumer-protection + self-regulation (ABSG advice 2021, upd. Nov 2024) | **VERIFIED** |
| **EU** | Parliament resolution on online-games consumer protection (TA-9-2023-0008); scrutiny under Unfair Commercial Practices Directive + DSA (dark patterns) | **VERIFIED** (resolution exists; it is non-binding) |
| **Australia** | 2025 classification: loot boxes → minimum **M**; simulated gambling → **R18+** | **VERIFIED** (Senet Group) |
| **US** | No federal loot-box statute; **FTC** acts under Section 5 (Epic $245M dark patterns + $275M COPPA; Cognosphere/Genshin Jan 2025 proposed order: block <16 loot-box purchase w/o parental consent, disclose odds + currency conversion) | **VERIFIED** (FTC.gov for Epic; Cognosphere via deep-research, **confirm exact terms in legal vector**) |

### 6.3 Odds disclosure & age-rating labels (VERIFIED)

- **Apple App Store:** loot-box odds disclosure required since **Dec 2017**.
- **Google Play:** loot-box odds disclosure required since **May 30 2019**.
- **ESRB:** "**In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)**" interactive element, introduced **Apr 2020**, covers loot boxes/gacha/card packs/prize wheels.
- **PEGI:** **12 Mar 2026** "interactive risk categories" reform — games selling **paid random items** receive **minimum PEGI 16** (effective for submissions from Jun 2026); time/quantity-limited offers flagged (the "PEGI 12 for limited offers" specific is from deep-research — **confirm in legal vector**).

### 6.4 Academic findings (peer-reviewed)

Multiple studies (Zendle & Cairns 2018; systematic reviews; JMIR Games 2024) find **loot-box spending correlates with problem-gambling severity** in adults and adolescents, with effect sizes comparable to known gambling risk factors. UK Gambling Commission "Young People & Gambling 2024" tracks minors paying for in-game items. **This is the empirical basis for treating loot boxes as a human/legal gate, not a free generation target.**

## 7. Machine-Simulatable vs Human-Strategic (the core split)

| Sub-area | Machine-specifiable / simulatable | Human-strategic | Simulation limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business model** | Archetype config; cohort P&L sim under priors; feasibility invariants | Value proposition; brand; "monetize at all?"; ethical stance | Market acceptance, PR/regulatory risk, trust |
| **Virtual economy** | Faucet/sink graph; conservation/inflation bounds; time-to-goal; pay-to-win gap; dual-currency consistency | What grind feels fair; diegetic fit; scarcity ethics | Emergent metas, trading, **whale/compulsion dynamics** |
| **IAP / bundles** | Catalog config; EV vs price; honest-best-value invariant; anchoring math | Which fantasies to sell; aggressiveness | True willingness-to-pay |
| **Battle pass** | Tier/reward schedule; completion-feasibility sim | Acceptable FOMO/time-pressure | Real perceived fairness |
| **Cosmetics** | **No-pay-to-win invariant** (verifiable) | Identity/status pressure tolerance | Cultural perception of price |
| **Gacha / loot box** | Drop tables; pity correctness; EV; cost-to-target bounds | **Whether to include at all**; disclosure UX; caps | Psychological/addiction impact |
| **Ads** | Type allow-list; frequency caps; rewarded-faucet economy fit; anti-mis-tap | Ad-load tolerance; partner brand safety | eCPM, real engagement impact |
| **Pricing** | Price lattice; regional matrix vs policy bounds; menu rationality | Fairness thresholds; dynamic-pricing yes/no | **Real elasticity (live A/B only)** |
| **Segmentation / LTV** | RFM/behavioral clusters; LTV/survival models; Gini/Lorenz | Which segmentation is *allowed*; targeting ethics | Healthy-vs-exploitative response |
| **Live-economy tuning** | A/B/bandit framework; guardrail KPIs; bounded auto-adjust | Which metrics matter; approve high-impact experiments; override anomalies | Lagged harms (addiction/regret) invisible to KPIs |

**The hard boundary (monetization's analog of "never auto-score fun"):** the factory may **search and optimize only within a human-authored policy envelope**. It may *measure* whale concentration, LTV, and elasticity; it may **never adopt "maximize LTV/ARPU" as an unconstrained objective**, because that objective provably converges on dark patterns. See §10.

## 9. Genre Variation

Monetization is *more* genre-coupled than most disciplines — the wrong model breaks the genre.

| Genre | Dominant model | Dominant monetization artifact | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Det-sim pilot** (roguelike / sim / management / factory) | **Premium** or premium + cosmetic DLC | Price lattice; optional cosmetic catalog | **Lowest-risk surface** — aligns pilot with minimal ethical/regulatory exposure |
| **Idle / incremental** | F2P + ads + IAP | Production/prestige formula + rewarded-ad faucet | Almost pure math; highly simulatable economy |
| **Mobile puzzle / match-3** | Hybrid (IAP + ads) | Difficulty/energy curve + offer schedule | Funnel/FTUE-driven; A/B-heavy |
| **MOBA / hero shooter** | **Cosmetics-only F2P** | Cosmetic catalog + battle pass; **no-pay-to-win invariant** | The "ethical" model; massive-scale dependent |
| **Battle royale** | Cosmetics + battle pass | Pass schedule + shop rotation | FOMO-forward; FTC-scrutinized (Epic) |
| **Gacha RPG** | F2P + gacha + passes | **Drop tables + pity + currency graph** | **Highest regulatory exposure** — gacha gate mandatory |
| **CCG / deckbuilder** | F2P + pack IAP | Pack drop tables + dust/craft economy | Pack-opening = loot-box-adjacent (disclosure) |
| **MMO** | Sub and/or B2P + cash shop | Sub tiers + cosmetic/QoL cash shop + economy sinks | Economy-sink design is load-bearing (inflation) |
| **Narrative / adventure** | **Premium** (often) | Price + DLC/episodes | Retention is a *misleading* monetization proxy (cf. qa-testing-liveops §9) |

**Implication:** `business-model-spec` and the monetization-mechanic allow-list are **genre-profile parameters**, exactly like `determinism_tier_target` and `playtest_instruments`. The factory must not hard-code one monetization model any more than it hard-codes one success function.

## 10. Factory Artifacts / Contracts This Discipline Implies

Engine-neutral, design-fused. Several **extend existing design contracts** rather than introducing parallel machinery.

1. **`business-model-spec`** — archetype (premium/F2P/hybrid/sub/ad/web3-off), permitted revenue streams + target weights, platform/regulatory constraints, funnel definitions + conversion/retention priors. Validated by cohort-P&L feasibility sim.

2. **`monetization-spec`** — top-level monetization intent + typed references into the artifacts below (the monetization analog of the federated `design-spec`).

3. **`live-economy-balance-contract`** — **extension of `economy-balance-contract`** (game-design-discipline.md §6) with monetization faucets (IAP, rewarded ads) and premium sinks (gacha, passes). Invariants: source/sink conservation, bounded inflation, **no-progression-deadlock-without-spend**, **pay-to-win-gap ≤ K%** (or **= 0** for cosmetics-only). Validated by Machinations-style sim + property tests.

4. **`sink-faucet-model`** — the executable economy graph (nodes = resources, edges = flows incl. real-money faucets) + seeded player-archetype distributions, run as Monte-Carlo for inflation/time-to-goal/whale-gap.

5. **`pricing-matrix`** — price lattice (price points, edition tiers, anchors), regional matrix (per Steam-style region groups / mobile tiers), discount bands + cooldowns. Validated by menu-rationality + regional-policy-bound checks.

6. **`iap-catalog`** — SKUs (consumable/durable/sub), bundles, starter packs, offers; with EV + honest-best-value invariants.

7. **`battle-pass-spec`** — tier/XP/reward schedule, free-vs-paid tracks, premium-plus; validated by completion-feasibility sim.

8. **`gacha-spec`** — drop tables, pity/mercy thresholds, featured/50-50 rules, duplicate conversion; validated by EV + cost-to-target + pity-correctness; **inclusion gated by `monetization-ethics-contract` + human sign-off**.

9. **`ad-monetization-spec`** — ad-type allow-list per placement hook, frequency caps/cooldowns, mediation wiring; rewarded faucets feed `sink-faucet-model`; anti-mis-tap invariant.

10. **`monetization-ethics-contract`** — **THE KEY NEW ARTIFACT.** Human-authored, machine-checkable policy envelope: transparency requirements (real-money-equivalent + odds disclosure), no-deception/no-mis-tap rules, bounded pressure (prompts/session, per-day spend friction, max expected cost-to-target), minor/vulnerable protections (spend caps, no-loot-box-for-minors, no-P2W-in-ranked), permitted-mechanic allow-list, regional regulatory map (loot-box-off where banned). **Every generated monetization config must pass this as a hard gate** — the monetization analog of a Behavioral Contract.

11. **`segmentation-ltv-spec`** — segmentation logic (RFM/behavioral), LTV/survival model config, **with anti-vulnerability-targeting invariants** (no offer escalation by inferred vulnerability; no protected-attribute targeting). Validated offline on synthetic/historic data.

12. **`liveops-economy-runbook`** — **extension of `liveops-runbook`** (qa-testing-liveops.md §10): A/B + bandit framework over monetization params, **guardrail KPIs** (refund/complaint rate, spend-concentration Gini, minor-spend, wellbeing proxies), bounded auto-adjust rules, human-gate predicates for high-impact experiments, auto-rollback on guardrail breach.

**Convergence-dimension impact:** monetization adds an **`economy-monetization-health`** sub-dimension to the existing convergence model — machine-checkable (economy invariants + ethics-contract pass) for the spine, **human-gated** for the strategic shell. The KPI-dashboard-spec (qa §10 #7) and telemetry-event-taxonomy gain explicit economy/monetization events, **labeled "machine-verifiable revenue-health, NOT fun and NOT welfare."**

## 11. AAA Acceptance Bar

To claim "AAA monetization-ready," a factory output must clear:

- **Economy health (machine):** faucet/sink conservation holds over a long-horizon sim; bounded inflation; no unintended progression deadlock; pay-to-win gap within declared bound (or zero for cosmetics-only).
- **Mechanic correctness (machine):** every IAP bundle's "best value" tag is honest; gacha EV + pity guarantees verified; battle pass completable by a normal player without coerced spend; ad frequency within caps; rewarded faucet doesn't break economy conservation.
- **Pricing integrity (machine):** price menu rational (no deceptive dominated SKUs); regional matrix within policy bounds; kid-directed caps respected.
- **Ethics-contract pass (machine, hard gate):** zero dark-pattern invariant violations; required disclosures present (odds, real-money-equivalent); bounded-pressure limits met; minor/vulnerable protections active; loot-box-off in banned regions.
- **Regulatory pre-flight (machine + legal-vector handoff):** loot-box/odds-disclosure obligations satisfied per target region; age-rating implications (ESRB/PEGI random-items labels) flagged to the ratings/legal vector.
- **Strategic shell (HUMAN gate, mandatory, non-automatable):** a human signs off on the business model, the value proposition, the acceptable level of monetization pressure/FOMO, and the inclusion of any high-risk mechanic (gacha, energy timers, loss-triggered offers). **This gate cannot be automated — it is the explicit boundary, exactly as playtest-satisfaction is for fun.**

## 12. Scope Implications for the Product Brief

The current brief (`.factory/specs/product-brief.md`) treats monetization only implicitly. Findings imply:

- **SI-1 (monetization is in-scope as a discipline, parameterized by genre).** The brief's design/economy lane already produces `economy-graph`/`balance-data`; monetization is a thin, high-value *extension* of that same simulatable spine plus a new ethics-contract gate. It should be named explicitly, not left implicit. **Most genres the factory targets have a monetization layer; omitting it makes outputs non-shippable.**
- **SI-2 (a new `monetization-ethics-contract` is required and is load-bearing).** This is the single most important addition. Without it, a lights-out factory optimizing economy/LTV will generate dark patterns by construction. It is the monetization analog of R-010 ("fun cannot be auto-scored → compulsion-loop risk") — **and it operationalizes R-010's mitigation.** The brief's Out-of-Scope "automatically scoring fun" should be paired with a new principle: **"the factory never adopts an unconstrained revenue objective; monetization is constrained optimization within a human ethics envelope."**
- **SI-3 (business-model and monetization-mechanic allow-list are genre-profile parameters)** alongside `determinism_tier_target` and `playtest_instruments` (genre matrix, game-design-discipline.md §11 / qa-testing-liveops.md §9).
- **SI-4 (det-sim pilot is also the lowest-monetization-risk pilot).** Roguelike/sim/management are typically premium or premium+cosmetic — minimal gacha/regulatory exposure. This **strengthens** the existing pilot choice: it maximizes the verifiable spine AND minimizes monetization-ethics surface simultaneously.
- **SI-5 (loot-box/gambling regulation cross-references the ratings/legal vector).** The factory needs a machine-readable regional regulatory map as input to the ethics contract; the *authoring* of that map (statute interpretation) is the legal vector's job. Recorded here as a dependency, not duplicated.
- **SI-6 (Web3/play-to-earn default OUT),** recorded in the risk register with AI-music (R-003) as litigation/regulatory/PR-exposed.
- **SI-7 (wrap Machinations + a mediation SDK; build the schemas + verifiers).** Consistent with the wrap-don't-build discipline; no new heavy subsystem.

## 13. Open Questions / Risks

- **OQ-M1 (ethics-contract authorship):** the `monetization-ethics-contract` is human-authored policy. Does the factory ship a *default* ethics envelope (e.g., "cosmetics-only, no loot boxes, no minor targeting, full disclosure") that studios relax explicitly, or require studios to author it from scratch? A safe default is strongly recommended.
- **OQ-M2 (live-data dependency):** real elasticity, whale dynamics, and segmentation response are only knowable from live A/B data. The factory can *generate and simulate* a monetization design but cannot *validate* its real-world revenue/welfare without live operation. Where is the boundary between factory-generated (pre-live) and liveops-tuned (post-live)?
- **OQ-M3 (regulatory map maintenance):** the regional regulatory map versions frequently (PEGI 12 Mar 2026; Australia 2025; FTC 2025 actions). Like cert frameworks (R-012), it cannot be a one-time build — it needs a maintenance cadence and a clean handoff to the legal vector.
- **R-M1 (confabulation watch):** several specifics here were flagged — Netherlands annulment **[UNVERIFIED]**, gacha exact rates **[illustrative]**, market-size/ARPU/whale-% **[vendor estimates]**, "spend 15–25% more on premium currency" **[directional]**. Consistent with AAA-RECONCILIATION R-009: **do not treat flagged figures as load-bearing.** Verified items (FTC $245M, Belgium 2018, ESRB Apr 2020, Google Play May 2019 / Apple Dec 2017, PEGI 12 Mar 2026) are primary-confirmed.
- **R-M2 (optimization drift):** even with constraints, a bandit/optimizer can find adversarial parameterizations near constraint boundaries. The ethics contract needs *margin*, and the liveops guardrails need lagged-harm proxies — KPIs alone miss addiction/regret. This is an active design risk, not a solved problem.
